How it measures

Distance (measured): the app finds the flagstick, locates its top and base to a fraction of a pixel across several frames, and turns its size in the image into distance using the lens's focal length. Each result comes with a 95% range and a confidence rating. When the range is wide, the app shows a range instead of a single number.

Elevation (sensor): the angle to the base of the pin from the tilt sensor, combined with the measured distance. It's only as good as the tilt calibration.

Adjustments (estimates): slope 1:1 by default; wind +1% per mph into, −0.5% per mph helping, 0.45% drift per mph across; air density from station temperature, pressure and humidity vs 70°F at your home elevation. These are rules of thumb, not ball-flight physics.

Privacy: camera frames are processed on the phone and never uploaded. Only your rounded location (about 1 km) goes to Open-Meteo, for weather.

Calibrate this lens

Measure an object of known height at a known horizontal distance (a pin at a measured yardage, or confirm with a laser). The app works out the lens focal length from it. Each save replaces the last. No tape measure? Use Quick calibrate in Settings instead.

Calibrate tilt sensor

Press the back of the phone flat against a door frame or wall, upright, as if aiming the camera into it. Walls are vertical to within a fraction of a degree, so any reading is sensor bias. Tap Start and hold still for 3 seconds.

Using PinSpective

  1. Hold the phone upright with both hands. Put the crosshair on the flag (the cloth at the top of the stick). Green brackets appear when the stick is found, with a live distance. If it locks onto something else, tap the flag on screen.
  2. Use 2× or 4× to aim at distant flags. On iPhone, switching to the telephoto lens in Settings gives far better accuracy past 120 yd.
  3. Tap MEASURE and hold still for half a second. Several frames are combined.
  4. Check the red and blue lines on the frozen frame. Drag them if needed (pinch to zoom).
  5. Read the actual distance (measured), the slope and wind adjustments (estimates) and the plays-like number with its range.

Before your first round: calibrate the tilt sensor, then the lens. Both are in ⚙ Settings. Quick calibrate needs no tape measure, just a distant point to aim at.
